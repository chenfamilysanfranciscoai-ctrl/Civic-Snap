Understanding the issues

Volunteering goes further when you know why the work matters. Each of these causes has events or groups on Civic Snap.

Poverty and the cost of living

Living costs in the Bay Area are among the highest in the country, and many working families still struggle to cover rent and food. PPIC reports that California's poverty rate fell to 10.5% in 2021 and rose to 17.4% in 2024, partly as pandemic-era aid ended and living costs climbed. Tipping Point's 2025 report found Bay Area poverty rising, with San Francisco's rate the highest of any county in the region.

Homelessness and housing

San Francisco's one-night count on January 30, 2024 found 8,323 people experiencing homelessness: 4,354 unsheltered and 3,969 in shelters. That total was up 7% from 2022. A single-night count is only a snapshot, and it cannot capture everyone who loses housing over a year. Groups here serve meals, repair homes, build, and run shelters.

Hunger and food access

Second Harvest of Silicon Valley's 2026 client survey found that 96% of the people it surveyed are cutting back on food to afford other necessities, and the food bank says it serves about 1 in 6 residents each month. Sorting, packing and distributing food is some of the most reliable volunteer work there is, with shifts almost every day.

Disaster preparedness

The Bay Area sits on active earthquake faults and faces wildfire smoke and flooding. Free neighborhood response training, like San Francisco's NERT and local CERT classes, teaches ordinary people to help in the first hours before professionals arrive.

SF NERT
